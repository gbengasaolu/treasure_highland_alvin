Her story, one chapter at a time

Tap each card as you read it.

1

A girl from the Bronx

Sonia Maria Sotomayor was born on June 25, 1954, in the Bronx, a part of New York City. Her parents were born in Puerto Rico. Sonia grew up in a Puerto Rican family in New York.

2

She faced hard things

When Sonia was seven, she learned that she had type 1 diabetes and began taking insulin. Her father died when she was nine. Her mother believed strongly in education and encouraged Sonia and her brother to learn.

3

A dream to work in law

As a child, Sonia liked the detective Nancy Drew. Later, a television show about a lawyer helped interest her in law. By about age ten, Sonia said she knew she wanted to go to college and become an attorney.

4

She studied and worked hard

Sonia did very well in school. She went to Princeton University. At first, college was difficult for her, so she spent long hours studying. She improved and graduated with high honors. Then she went to Yale Law School.

5

Lawyer, then judge

After law school, Sonia worked as an assistant district attorney in New York. Later she became a federal judge. She served on the U.S. District Court and then the U.S. Court of Appeals.

6

A historic achievement

In 2009, President Barack Obama nominated Sonia Sotomayor to the U.S. Supreme Court. The Senate confirmed her, and she began serving on August 8, 2009. She became the first Hispanic justice on the U.S. Supreme Court and the third woman to serve as a Supreme Court justice.

Big idea: Sonia Sotomayor's story shows how education, hard work, public service, and persistence can shape a person's life. When school became difficult, she kept learning and improving.
